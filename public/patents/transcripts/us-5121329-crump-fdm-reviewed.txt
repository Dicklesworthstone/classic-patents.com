This invention relates to an apparatus and process for forming a three-dimensional article from a computer-aided design (CAD) database. More particularly, the invention is directed to an apparatus and method for creating three-dimensional physical objects of a predetermined shape by sequentially depositing multiple layers of solidifying material on a base member in a desired pattern, as illustrated in FIG. 1.

In the preferred embodiment shown in FIG. 1 and FIG. 2, a movable dispensing head 10 incorporates a heated liquefier flow passage 20 terminating in a dispensing outlet comprising a calibrated discharge nozzle tip 22. A solid flexible strand or filament 12 is drawn from supply reel 14 by motor-driven pinch feed rollers 28 and driven into liquefier 20 under positive pressure.

As depicted in FIG. 3 and FIG. 4, the dispensing head 10 is maintained in close, working proximity to base member 18 or the preceding layer. The planar bottom face of discharge tip 22 provides a continuous shearing and flattening effect on the top surface of the dispensed thermoplastic bead 40, ensuring precise layer thickness control and eliminating accumulative Z-axis height errors.

Referring to FIG. 5 and FIG. 6, each successive planar cross-section is fabricated by depositing a continuous closed perimeter contour bead followed by back-and-forth raster vector infill passes 46. The newly extruded molten bead adheres and thermally welds to the preceding layer upon cooling below its solidification temperature, building up solid, dense, high-strength articles.

As shown in FIG. 11, CAD solid model data is sliced into parallel cross-sectional layers, and machine-level G-code drive signals are computed to actuate X, Y, and Z stepper motors in coordination with filament feed velocity. Free-space wireframe geometries (FIG. 12) or multiple-orifice extrusion manifolds (FIG. 7 and FIG. 8) may also be utilized within the scope of the invention.
