This invention relates to miniature electronic circuits, and more particularly to unique integrated electronic circuits fabricated from semiconductor material.
Many methods and techniques for miniaturizing electronic circuits have been proposed in the past. At first, most of the effort was spent upon reducing the size of the components and packing them more closely together. Work directed toward reducing component size is still continuing, but has reached a point where component handling and interconnecting problems limit further miniaturization. The large number of individual electrical connections required in complex systems creates a major reliability hazard known as the 'tyranny of numbers'.
In accordance with the principles of the present invention, the ultimate in circuit miniaturization is attained by fabricating all active and passive components of an entire electronic circuit within a single monolithic body of semiconductor material, utilizing only one material and compatible process steps.
This is accomplished by utilizing a body of semiconductor material exhibiting one conductivity type (either n-type or p-type) and having formed therein diffused regions forming p-n junctions. Resistors are provided by the bulk resistivity of shaped semiconductor paths, capacitors are provided by reverse-biased p-n junctions or dielectric surface coatings, and transistors and diodes are formed by mesa diffused and alloyed regions.

Referring to the drawings, in FIG. 1 there is shown a resistor formed from a wafer of semiconductor material such as silicon or germanium. The resistance value is determined by the length, cross-sectional area, and bulk resistivity of the semiconductor path between ohmic contacts.
In FIG. 2, an integrated capacitor is formed utilizing the transition capacitance of a reverse-biased p-n junction. The capacitance per unit area is controlled by the doping gradient and applied reverse bias voltage. Alternatively, a thin silicon oxide dielectric layer is formed on the semiconductor surface and covered by an evaporated conductive metal film.
In FIGS. 3 and 4, an active bipolar junction transistor is integrated into the body. A collector region is provided by the semiconductor substrate, a base region is formed by vapor diffusion of opposite-type impurities, and an emitter region is formed by an alloyed dot or second diffusion.
In FIGS. 5 and 6, a distributed resistance-capacitance network is formed by a continuous p-n junction where the upper layer provides a distributed resistive path and the junction provides distributed capacitance to ground.

--- REVIEWED TRANSCRIPTION PAGE 7 OF 9 ---
Referring to FIGS. 7 and 8a-8c, a complete bistable multivibrator (flip-flop) circuit is fabricated within a single rectangular bar of semiconductor material having dimensions of only approximately 0.200 by 0.080 by 0.010 inches.
The bar includes two diffused mesa transistors, two cross-coupling capacitors, two collector load resistors, and four base biasing resistors.
Selective mesa etching isolates the individual component regions into mesas extending above the main substrate. Interconnections between components are made by gold flying-wire thermal compression bonds or evaporated conductive film overlays.
Power supply potentials (+Vcc, -Vbb, Ground) and input/output trigger terminals are connected to peripheral contact areas. When operating, the circuit functions as a complete digital binary counter stage.

A further illustration of the invention is shown in FIGS. 11 and 12, illustrating a complete phase-shift audio oscillator integrated within a single semiconductor wafer. The wafer contains a mesa transistor amplifier, bulk load resistors, and a distributed RC phase-shift feedback network producing self-sustained sinusoidal oscillations.
Using the techniques of the present invention, complete operational electronic circuits are produced with component packing densities exceeding 10,000,000 parts per cubic foot, while eliminating hundreds of soldered wire connections.
